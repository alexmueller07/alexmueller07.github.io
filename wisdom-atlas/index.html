Where modeled opinion meets measured outcomes

This is an unofficial proof of concept for the WISDOM correlation project. It joins the 25 county estimates published in the Wisconsin Diversity Opinion Maps with outside measures of health, segregation, social capital, mobility and policing, and lets you test how they relate. It is not a product of the Institute for Diversity Science.

Read this before quoting a number

  1. WISDOM values are modeled, not measured. One national survey of 4,775 adults was turned into estimates for 3,144 counties, about 1.5 respondents per county. The model predicts each county from the age, sex, race, education, income and 2024 vote of the people who live there, plus county education, diversity and density. A county's value mostly reflects who lives there and how it voted.
  2. So a raw correlation can be built in. Any outcome that tracks vote, education or race will correlate with WISDOM for that reason alone. Use Hold constant to see what is left once those are removed. Often little is.
  3. Counties are not independent. Neighbors resemble each other, so ordinary p-values overstate certainty. The atlas reports intervals from resampling whole states and prints no p-values.
  4. Places are not people. A link between counties does not show that the individuals who hold an opinion are the ones who experience an outcome.
  5. WISDOM publishes no margins of error. Small differences between two counties are not established differences in opinion.
  6. Small counts are noisy. Race-specific health measures and police shootings are missing or unstable in small counties. Check the count of places under every result.

Sources

State values come from each source where it publishes them (WISDOM, Census, County Health Rankings, election returns). For county-only sources they are population-weighted county averages.

How the reference papers show up here

PaperWhat it didIn this atlas
Leitner et al. (2016)County racial attitudes against Black and White health outcomes.White–Black life expectancy gap and Black-to-White premature death ratio, with covariates you can hold constant.
Hehman et al. (2018)Regional bias against lethal force that is out of proportion to the Black population share, beyond demographics.Black over-representation in fatal police shootings, 2015–2024, built from the Washington Post database.
Zhang et al. (2023)Racialized economic segregation (ICE) against county mortality, with state clustering and population weights.ICE computed from the 2020–2024 ACS, a population-weight switch, within-state comparison and state-clustered intervals.
Chetty et al. (2022)Social capital measured from 21 billion friendships.Economic connectedness, exposure, friending bias, clustering, support ratio, volunteering and civic organizations.
Henderson et al. (2021)County counts of Confederate memorials against lynching victims in 11 Southern states.Not loaded yet. The memorial and lynching counts are the next layer to add.

Not in this prototype yet

  • Project Implicit county averages for the race, sexuality and disability IATs, which are the direct comparison the reference papers make. The public files are individual responses and need their own aggregation step.
  • FBI hate crime counts, Confederate memorial counts and historical lynching records.
  • Uncertainty for the WISDOM estimates and checks against direct state survey averages, which need the respondent-level file.
